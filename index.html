Chandramouli Rajagopalan

Research Engineer · Astera Institute

My work spans learning in simulation, aligning perception and control interfaces, and deploying policies on physical robots. Previously, I received an M.S. in Electrical Engineering (Intelligent Systems, Robotics, and Control) from UC San Diego, where I co-authored work on adapting offline world models in the real world.

I’m interested in how robots can learn goal-conditioned skills, reuse them across tasks, and adapt when the world changes.

Research interests: offline reinforcement learning, world models, goal-conditioned and reusable skills, and sim-to-real manipulation.

Research & projects

Offline learning

Finetuning offline world models

UC San Diego · CoRL 2023 Oral · Published research

Pretrain a world model on offline robot data, then adapt it through online interaction. Test-time planning balances estimated return with model uncertainty to reduce extrapolation errors.

My contributions included simulation environments, simulation and task-transfer experiments, and corresponding real-robot experiments.

Paper Project Code

Value-Guided Action Transport

Offline reinforcement learning · Ongoing research

I led the development of Value-Guided Action Transport, which refines actions proposed by a behavior-cloned policy toward higher estimated value. I evaluated the method on twelve D4RL tasks spanning navigation, locomotion, and dexterous manipulation.

Robotics systems and manipulation

Sim-to-real insertion and skill composition

Astera Institute · Imitation and reinforcement learning

Trained camera-based insertion students with DAgger and deployed them on a high torque robotics arm. In simulation, I also trained specialist policies and a router for OpenArm stacking and insertion. Current research questions include local insertion search and transfer across starting conditions and part geometry.

Grasp generation and recoverable picking

Astera Institute · Diffusion models and scripted recovery

I fine-tuned a diffusion grasp generator using grasps that held through lifting and rotation in simulation. In a separate physical picking system, I integrated a pretrained grasp detector with a reaching controller I co-developed with a teammate. A scripted skill graph checked the outcome of each attempt and could retry a failed grasp.
